But how, /exactly/, do you actually /do/ that deep work? 

How do you work through those hard problems? How do you manage your mind through all those blank pages that keep staring at you? How do you think your way through complex issues with lots of moving parts?

*** For that, *you need a /writer/*
Over a fifty-year career of writing about very hard things, and a lifetime of studying great writers, I've managed to figure out my /own/ mind. In the process, I've learned a ton of cognitive psychology.  

More importantly, I've also learned that what works for me will work for just about everybody.  

So far, I've put together just under thirty powerful tricks to make hard problems irresistible, all of them shamelessly approximated from my interest in how writers do things:

1. The Lamott Splatt: Start with a horrible strawman; fixing it will be irresistible.
2. The Bradbury Override: Drop rigid schedules when impulse strikes.
3. The Asimov Shuffle: Keep many projects open & switch when bored or stuck.
4. The Chandler Bear-Trap: Forbid all work to force boredom, which will eventually force action.
5. Gaiman's Permission: Give yourself permission to daydream.
6. The Cunningham Bypass: Plan exactly wrong, and watch yourself execute flawlessly.
7. Doctorow's Pacing: Kill overthinking and spinning thoughts with physical movement.
8. The Trollope Two-Step: Set a timer or a numeric goal to crowd out the censor.
9. Hugo's Restraint: Modify your environment to support work -- before you start.
10. Schiller's Apples: Add mood-influencing sensory triggers.
11. The Angelou Airlock: Enforce strict boundaries on your mental states.
12. London's Goat Path: Let the work lead you, not the other way around.
13. Christie's Guardrails: Keep notebooks of useful facts to constrain imagination.
14. Dickens' Mirrors: Inhabit people who could find the solution.
15. Stevenson Brownies: Set a distinct time or place to do something difficult.
16. The Hemingway Bridge: Quit at peak momentum in mid-task and restart tomorrow.
17. The Twain Scratchpad: Capture unrelated or tangential thoughts for later.
18. Nabokov's Puzzles: Break big problems into steps and rearrange them until things make sense.
19. The Luhmann Scramble: Unlink and relink ideas until things feel smooth and realistic.
20. Rowling's Countdown: Work backwards from the end goal, one backstep at a time.
21. The Heller Grid: Build a map of desired state changes all along the path from start to finish.
22. The Caro-Eco Web: Expand a complex problem into a mind-map to find hidden connections.
23. The Faulkner Muzzle: Let different parts of your brain each weigh in on a problem /in turn/.
24. The Steinbeck Jolt: Use a physical or verbal anomaly to break unresolvable loops.
25. Quiller's Knife: Enforce a strict contract or perimeter on problems and solutions.
26. King's Guillotine: Kill your darlings; don't let /clever/ stand in for /correct/.
27. Abraham Lincoln's Reset: Invest time in your tools, and you'll feel committed to start.
28. Salinger's Doubts: Treat much of your thinking as if the narrator is naive.
29. The Wallace Hedge: Mark all unqualified opinions with "[CITATION NEEDED]."
