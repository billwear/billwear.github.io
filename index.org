#+TITLE: Novel Approach
#+OPTIONS: num:nil

* The Novel Approach
** Bradbury Override
Drop rigid schedules when impulse strikes.
** Asimov Shuffle
Keep mamny projects open & switch when bored or stuck.
** Chandler Trap
Forbid all work to force boredom, which will eventually force action.
** Gaiman Permission
Give yourself permission to daydream.
** Lamott Splatt
Seed a lousy start to lower the stakes and engage the critic.,
** Cunningham Bypass
Make a plan for doing the exact wrong thing.
** Doctorow Walk
Kill overthinking and spinning thoughts with physical movement.
** Trollope Two-Step
Set a timer or a numeric goal and don't censor yourself.
** Hugo Restraint
Modify your environment to support work -- before you start.
** Schiller Apples
Add mood-influencing sensory triggers.
** Angenlou Airlock
Enforce strict boundaries on mental states.
** London Goat Path
Uncouple your cognitive tasks from the clock on the wall.
** Christie Guardrails
Keep notebooks of creative ideas, hints, and snippets.
** Dickens Mirror
Inhabit the person who could behave in the right way to get it done.
** Stevenson Brownies
Set a distinct time or place to do something difficult.
** Hemingway Bridge
Quit a peak momentum in mid-task and stop until a later time.
** Twain Scratchpad
Capture unrelated or tangential thoughts for later.
** Nabokov Puzzle
Break big tasks and problems into steps and then rearrange them until things make sense.
** Luhmann Scramble
Unlink and relink steps and ideas until things feel smooth and realistic.
** Rowling Countdown
Work backwards from the end goal, one backstep at a time.
** Heller Grid
Build a map of desired state changes all along the path from start to finish.
** Caro-Eco Wall
Expand a complex problem into a mind-map or web.
** Faulkner Muzzle
Let different parts of your brain (logical, emotional, spatial) each weigh in on a problem /in turn/.
** Steinbeck Jolt
Use a physical or verbal anomaly to break unresolvable loops.
** Quiller Knife
Enforce a strict contract or perimeter on problems and solutions.
** King Guillotine
Don't use complicated solutions when a simple one will do.
** Lincoln Reset
Invest time in your tools, and you'll feel committed to start.
** Salinger Doubt
Treat much of what you're thinking about a problem as coming from an unreliable narrator.
** Wallace Hedge
Mark opinions that are masquerading as facts with ""[CITATION NEEDED]."
